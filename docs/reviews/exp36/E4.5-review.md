## Normalized English content

### Identity and objectives

**Experiment 36:** Titration between Na₂CO₃ and HCl using phenolphthalein and
methyl orange.

The stated aims are to learn the shape of the titration curve for titrating
Na₂CO₃ with HCl and to investigate the pH ranges of the equivalence points
using suitable indicators.

The source describes two stages of carbonate neutralisation:

1. H⁺ converts CO₃²⁻ to HCO₃⁻.
2. H⁺ converts HCO₃⁻ to H₂CO₃.

It states that the two equivalence points have different pH characteristics.

### Apparatus and chemicals

The equipment table names a 250 cm³ volumetric flask, pipette, and burette.
The procedure additionally uses a plastic weighing dish and a titration flask.

The procedure names solid Na₂CO₃, distilled water, approximately 0.100 mol dm⁻³
HCl, phenolphthalein, and methyl orange. Methyl orange is present in the
procedure and endpoint discussion but is not clearly present in the extracted
chemical list.

### Procedure

1. Weigh 0.20–0.22 g Na₂CO₃ accurately to within 0.1 mg into a plastic
   weighing dish.
2. Transfer the solid to a 250 cm³ volumetric flask.
3. Pipette 50.00 cm³ distilled water into the flask.
4. Rinse remaining solid from the dish into the flask with pipette water,
   stir to dissolve, and make the solution up to the mark with distilled
   water.
5. Pipette approximately 50 cm³ of the prepared solution into a titration
   flask and add 4–5 drops of phenolphthalein.
6. Rinse a 50 cm³ burette with a few cm³ of approximately 0.100 mol dm⁻³ HCl.
7. Fill the burette with HCl and remove air bubbles from its tip.
8. Add HCl slowly to the titration flask.
9. Stop at the phenolphthalein endpoint when the solution becomes colourless
   and record the burette reading.
10. Add a few drops of methyl orange and continue adding HCl.
11. Stop when the solution becomes red and record the second burette reading.
12. Repeat the titration at least three times, average the endpoint volumes,
    and tabulate the trial data.

### Endpoint observations

- Phenolphthalein: pink to colourless; the source associates this transition
  with pH 9.8.
- Methyl orange: orange to red; the source associates this transition with
  pH 3.8.
- The source instructs warming near the second equivalence point to avoid the
  effect of CO₂.

### Questions and calculations

The source includes a post-lab question asking for readings for two 50 cm³
samples of 0.2 mol dm⁻³ Na₂CO₃ titrated with 0.2 mol dm⁻³ HCl using methyl
orange and phenolphthalein. A second question asks for the OH⁻:Na₂CO₃ ratio

These questions are source content only. They are not yet answer keys or

## Scientific calculation review

The calculation record is intentionally conditional. The handbook does not
provide a complete worked result or accepted endpoint-volume range for the
0.20–0.22 g preparation.

For sample mass `m`, sodium carbonate molar mass `M = 105.99 g mol⁻¹`
(rounded to 106 g mol⁻¹ for school-level arithmetic), and final preparation
volume `Vₚ = 0.250 dm³`:

```text
n(Na₂CO₃) = m / M
c(Na₂CO₃) = n / Vₚ
```

If the aliquot volume is confirmed as `Vₐ = 0.05000 dm³`, then:

```text
nₐ(Na₂CO₃) = c(Na₂CO₃) × Vₐ
n(HCl) at first endpoint = nₐ(Na₂CO₃)
n(HCl) at second endpoint = 2 × nₐ(Na₂CO₃)
```

For an HCl concentration `cₐ`:

```text
V₁(HCl) = nₐ(Na₂CO₃) / cₐ
V₂,cumulative(HCl) = 2 × nₐ(Na₂CO₃) / cₐ
```

For each trial `i`, the recorded readings should be represented as:

```text
V₁,i = first_endpoint_reading_i - initial_burette_reading_i
V₂,i = second_endpoint_reading_i - initial_burette_reading_i
```

The arithmetic mean is:

```text
mean(V₁) = ΣV₁,i / k
mean(V₂) = ΣV₂,i / k
```

where `k ≥ 3` only if the source instruction “at least three times” is
implemented. These are derived calculation relationships, not official
handbook results. The exact mass, aliquot precision, HCl concentration,
accepted endpoint readings, and grading tolerances require teacher approval.

The 50.00 cm³ initial water addition cannot be treated as the final solution
volume: the procedure says to make the preparation up to the 250 cm³ mark
after dissolution. The initial water is therefore a dissolution/transfer
step; the final volumetric-flask mark is the conditional preparation volume.
